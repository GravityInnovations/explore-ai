# Grade-level authoring guardrails

Use the learner's configured grade band as guidance, not as a universal reading formula. Grades 1–2 should use short, concrete sentences and no more than two simultaneous labelled targets. Grades 3–5 can handle four targets while introducing one new concept per step. Grades 6–8 can handle six targets and up to two connected new concepts when the relationship is explicit.

Every step should carry one primary learning point, introduce vocabulary before a later step uses it, and reconnect the final step to the objective. Keep prerequisites visible in the teaching sequence. The deterministic critic reports target overload, new-concept overload, vocabulary ordering, missing recap and obvious objective coverage gaps as warnings; it does not rewrite copy or use a single readability score as a pass/fail gate.

Quiz contracts must use the same objective and taught-step vocabulary once quiz support is added. A quiz item that assesses an untaught concept is a structural error, while richer detail for Grades 6–8 remains valid when the sequence explains it.
